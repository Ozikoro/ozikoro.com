One institution. Three public tools.

We keep history where people can find it.

Ozi Ikoro Limited publishes Ozikoro, Ozituma and the Academy: an archive, a living dictionary and a place to learn.

Our purpose

African histories should be readable, searchable and citable without being separated from the people who hold them.

The platform brings community testimony, scholarship, language and material culture into one research environment while keeping provenance visible.

Our name

Ozi Ikoro: the message of the drum

In Igbo communities the Ikoro, a great slit drum, carried messages across a town: gatherings, alarms, deaths of titled people, celebrations. Ozi means message. Ozi Ikoro Limited takes that role online — carrying what communities know to anyone who needs it, with the source always attached.

What we publish

Histories, folklores, towns and research

  • Histories — sourced articles across Igbo and African history.
  • Folklores & myths — stories you can read or listen to.
  • Towns — profiles of communities and their quarters.
  • Publications — researcher papers, open or access-controlled.

  • Igbo calendar and cultural calendar.

Editorial method

How a record earns its place

  1. Submit

    Writers, researchers and knowledge holders deposit material with consent and access terms.

  2. Verify

    Editors check sources; expert reviewers assess evidence.

  3. Publish

    Each record gets a permanent, citable address and visible version history.

  4. Correct

    Anyone can propose a correction; changes are logged, never hidden.

FAQs

Questions people ask

Answers describe the intended platform; policies marked "to be confirmed" await Ozi Ikoro Limited.

What is Ozikoro?

An online archive and research platform for Igbo and wider African history, run by Ozi Ikoro Limited. It is an institution, not a blog: every record carries its sources.

Is Ozikoro free to read?

Articles, folklores, calendars, films and most records are free. Some research publications are access-controlled by their authors and can be requested.

How do I cite an Ozikoro record?

Every record has a permanent address. The citation guide gives formats for articles, photographs, recordings and publications.

Can I contribute a story, photograph or research?

Yes. Use Contribute. You choose access and reuse terms; editors review before publishing.

I found a mistake. How do I correct it?

Use the correction link on any record or the contribution page. Corrections are reviewed and logged in version history.

What is the difference between Ozikoro, Ozituma and Learn Igbo?

Ozikoro is the archive, Ozituma is the African-languages dictionary, and Learn Igbo teaches the language.

How are Igbo market days calculated?

The Igbo calendar follows the four-day Eke, Orie, Afọ, Nkwọ cycle. Communities may differ; local confirmation is advised.

How can I support the work?

Donate, sponsor a programme, explore investment or volunteer. Supporters appear on the public ledger with consent.

Where can I see your projects?

The projects page lists ongoing, planned and completed work.

Market week and ritual office: reading testimony against the administrative return

Chinwe Ị̀kẹ̀jìànị̀ (University of Nigeria, Nsukka) · Emeka Ǹwàchukwu (University of Port Harcourt)

Published 4 February 2026 · 28 pages · English, with Igbo-language appendix

Open access Precolonial market systems Oral historiography Igbo ritual office
Read full text (PDF, 1.4 MB) Download Cite

Abstract

Administrative returns from the early colonial period in southeastern Nigeria counted what they could measure — attendance, tolls, compounds — and interpreted what they could not. This paper sets those returns beside testimony recorded with town councils between 2024 and 2026 and asks a narrow question: where the two kinds of source describe the same thing, do they agree?

They largely do on reach and frequency, and they diverge sharply on authority. The paper argues that the divergence is not a problem of unreliable memory but of a recording apparatus built to register obligation as administration. A method is proposed for using such returns as quantitative evidence while setting aside their interpretive frame, and it is applied to four towns.
